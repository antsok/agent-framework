> **Superseded, 26 September 2026,** by the final report in `RESULTS.md` (runs 63 and 64): all
> twenty strategies at 0.9, 1.5 and 3x a 120K window on gpt-5.6-luna and gpt-6-luna.

## 1. The result

**Across every cell this project has ever measured, on either model, at any window size, fill
fraction or tool share, nothing is resolvably cheaper than not compacting.** Every negative
`vs none$` the instrument has printed has arrived with `NOT SUPPORTED` — the gap it rests on is
narrower than the spread between the seeds it is a median of.

That is not "compaction is expensive". It is narrower and more useful than that:

- **Retention and cost are separate questions, and only one of them has a good answer.** One
  strategy held every planted fact in every cell measured up to this report's date -- runs 41
  and 42; qualified at the top, it has lost facts on four of 28 records since. None makes the
  bill smaller.
- **Snapshot size does not predict cost. It frequently predicts it backwards.** The strategy that
  removed the most in run 42 is the strategy that cost the most.
- **The mechanism is the prompt cache.** A provider charges roughly a tenth for re-reading a
  prefix it has already seen. Compaction rewrites that prefix by construction, so the tokens it
  removes are saved at the cached rate while the tokens behind the edit are re-billed once at
  the full rate. On these workloads the second term wins.

The practical statement for a caller: **compact because the conversation would otherwise
overflow the window, not because you expect a smaller invoice.** That is what the instrument's
own verdict line says when it names `none`.

## 2. What the two runs are

Both ran on repaired code: the coverage check based on values rather than tool names, the record
protected from the fallback that used to shorten it, one record per trigger rather than two,
record repeats off by default, and the output reservation paid off (§9).

**Run 41 — the clean baseline.** Five arms, five seeds each, four rows per invocation
(`none,truncation,anchored,tool_summary_anchored`) so that `anchored` — the strategy
`tool_summary_anchored` falls back to — is compared on the *same conversations* rather than
across runs. 100 records, no throttling, no retries, no errors. Two models, two payload shapes
(a fixed 3,500-token result, and a result sized to an 80% tool share), and one arm with record
repeats on. `runs/run-41-*.txt`, `runs/run-41-clean-baseline.sh`.

**Run 42 — every strategy at one cell.** All eighteen registered strategies, `gpt-5.6-luna`,
60,000/0.86, five seeds, 90 records. `runs/run-42-luna-all-strategies.txt`,
`runs/run-42-all-strategies.sh`.

Each row of each table is a mean or median over five seeds, a seed being a whole conversation
driven from scratch. The `+-` columns are the spread between the cheapest and dearest seed, and
a ranking is only meaningful where the gap between two rows exceeds it.

## 3. Run 41: retention is the finding

| arm | `vs none$` | verdict | facts | `acc1` | `seed$+-` |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| luna, fixed payload | **-1%** | `tool_summary_anchored`, NOT SUPPORTED | 53/53 | 100% | 32% |
| luna, share 0.80 | **-3%** | `tool_summary_anchored`, NOT SUPPORTED | 53/53 | 100% | 19% |
| luna, share 0.80, repeats | +7% | `none` | 53/53 | 100% | 17% |
| mini, fixed payload | +25% | `none` | 53/53 | 100% | 79% |
| mini, share 0.80 | +10% | `none` | 53/53 | 96% | 33% |

**`tool_summary_anchored` held 53 of 53 planted facts in all 25 rows.** Recomputed from the
records rather than read off the tables: two models, two payloads, repeats on and off, coverage
succeeding and coverage failing, the post-record fallback firing and not firing. On the same
conversations, inside the same invocations, `anchored` read **35 to 53** and `truncation` **21 to
45**. No other strategy retains reliably, and this is the first run where that comparison is made
inside single invocations rather than between them.

**And the cost axis refuses to resolve.** Two arms name the record row, and the instrument prints
`NOT SUPPORTED` on both — a 1% gap against a 36% spread, and a 3% gap against a 19% one. Three
arms name `none`. So on `gpt-5.6-luna` the record sits at parity with not compacting, and on
`gpt-5.4-mini` it costs 10% to 25% more, which is the same answer every cell has given.

Those two negatives are the entire evidence for "cheaper", and neither is evidence. A reader who
takes -1% and -3% as savings is reading two numbers that the tool which produced them declines to
stand behind.

## 4. Run 42: size and cost run in opposite directions

Eighteen strategies in one cell is the first chance to see the whole ordering at once. `snap%` is
the snapshot every question was asked from as a share of the window — how hard compaction acted.
The uncompacted control sits at 81%.

| strategy | `snap%` | facts | `acc1` | `hit%` | `vs none$` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `none` | 81% | 53/53 | 100% | 96% | — |
| `tool_summary_anchored` | 56% | **53/53** | **100%** | 90% | -6% |
| `anchored_no_assistant` | 59% | 51/53 | 94% | 79% | +58% |
| `selective_tool_call` | 66% | 50/53 | 94% | 89% | +21% |
| `anchored` | 60% | 50/53 | 92% | 78% | +56% |
| `tool_result` | 71% | 49/53 | 93% | 89% | +33% |
| `anchored_min_gain` | 60% | 46/53 | 88% | 74% | +53% |
| `token_budget_summarize` | 30% | 40/53 | 76% | 73% | +25% |
| `context_window_lazy` | 60% | 36/53 | 69% | 88% | +29% |
| `context_window` | 40% | 24/53 | 46% | 81% | +1% |
| `truncation` | 39% | 20/53 | 39% | 82% | +20% |
| `token_budget_truncate_first` | 32% | 19/53 | 36% | 67% | +26% |
| `summarization` | 11% | 18/53 | 35% | 12% | **+104%** |
| `token_budget_fallback` | 34% | 18/53 | 35% | 33% | +98% |
| `token_budget_tools_first` | 32% | 17/53 | 34% | 71% | +26% |

| `token_budget_window_first` | 26% | 16/53 | 31% | 86% | -19% |
| `sliding_window` | 12% | 8/53 | 17% | 12% | +7% |

Three things fall out of it.

**`summarization` — the ladder the framework leads with — compacted the hardest and cost the
most.** It cut the snapshot to 11% of the window and charged 104% more than not compacting, at 18
of 53 facts: level with blind truncation, from a strategy that pays a second model to preserve.
Roughly $0.031 of its $0.070 seed cost is its own summarizer calls; the rest is the cache it
destroyed.

**`sliding_window` is the mechanism with nothing else attached.** 12% of the window, 8 of 53
facts, and still **7% dearer** than the control. It seeded 156,551 input tokens against the
control's 665,487 — less than a quarter — and its prompt side alone cost $0.0306 against the
control's $0.0225. Four times fewer tokens, a third more money, because 3% of its seeding tokens
were cache reads against the control's 92%.

**The row that removed the least of anything that acted is the row that kept everything.**
`tool_summary_anchored` at 56% against the control's 81% is a smaller edit than every strategy
below it in the `snap%` ordering, and it is the only one of the six rows that cleared the accuracy
bar to come in at or below the control on cost. `token_budget_window_first` reads -19%, which is
cheaper — at 16 of 53 facts and 31% accuracy. That is why the table ranks on correctness first,
and why a cost column read on its own is a trap.

**The cost column of run 42 is not usable and is printed here for shape only.** See §9.

## 5. The cache is the mechanism

The break-even is arithmetic, not intuition. With `p` the input rate, `c` the cached-read rate,
`R` the tokens removed, `B` the tokens sitting behind the edit and `T` the turns still to come:

> compaction pays when **`R > B(p − c) / (p + T·c)`**

At the roughly 10x discount both these models carry, with twenty turns left, that is **29% of the
tokens behind the edit** — not of the prompt, which it only resembles when the edit starts near
the front. Removing less than that costs more than it saves, however sensible the removal looks.

Run 42 is that inequality made visible. Order the rows by seeding-half cache hit rate and the
money follows it more closely than it follows size:

| | control | `tool_summary_anchored` | `token_budget_fallback` | `sliding_window` | `summarization` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| snapshot, share of window | 81% | 56% | 34% | 12% | 11% |
| seeding input tokens | 665,487 | 503,131 | 413,914 | 156,551 | 160,194 |
| of those, cache reads | 92% | 88% | 33% | 3% | 2% |
| prompt side, `seed in$` | $0.0225 | $0.0212 | $0.0582 | $0.0306 | $0.0315 |

Run 40 gives the same finding on the other model with the output half attached: `gpt-5.4-mini`
removed 17-22% of its snapshot and **cost 44% more**, because the hit rate fell from 81% to 68%
*and* output rose from 9,278 to 13,261 tokens at six times the input price. A smaller prompt
bought with a broken prefix and a longer answer is not a cheaper prompt.

**`snap%` is not a proxy for cost, and reading it as one gets the sign wrong.** That mistake was
published twice inside this project during runs 40 and 41, and is corrected here rather than
quietly.

## 6. The coverage check sits on a cliff

`tool_summary_anchored` will only delete a tool group whose values the record demonstrably
carries — `--coverage-share`, 0.8 by default, of the group's distinctive values quoted back.
Groups that fall short are kept, and counted as `UNCOVERED:<n>`.

Recomputed over run 41's 25 record rows: `UNCOVERED` was **zero in 15 of them**, and the two arms
where it was not are `mini, share 0.80` (four of five seeds fell short) and `luna, share 0.80`
(two of five).

Where it fails it fails completely, and shrink moves with it one-for-one. `mini, share 0.80`,
seeds 1 to 5:

| seed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `UNCOVERED` | 4 | 3 | **0** | 4 | 3 |
| snapshot removed, against the control | -2% | 9% | **41%** | -1% | 8% |

The one seed whose record covered everything removed 41% of the prompt. The four that fell short
removed between -2% and 9% — that is, two of them ended up with a *larger* snapshot than the
control, having paid for a record and an extra agent turn and compacted nothing.

The threshold is chosen, not derived, and its right value depends on how many values a workload's
results carry: at the eight per result these runs use, 0.8 tolerates exactly one unrecognisable
value; at two values per group the share can only be 0, 0.5 or 1. **`--coverage-share` is now
reachable from the CLI, and sweeping it is the single most useful thing to run next.**

## 7. Repeats are payload- and model-dependent, and default off

`--record-repeats` lets the size trigger ask for a second record once new tool work has
accumulated that no existing record accounts for. Run 41 measured it on one arm.

On `gpt-5.6-luna` at share 0.80, repeats cut the snapshot **62-66%** against the same arm's
29-40% without them, and the cross-cell report puts the repeats arm at **$0.0266 against
$0.0240** — 10% dearer on the same workload, with the same 53/53 retention. **The report's own
guard prints `NOT RESOLVED` on that 10%**, against a 19% seed spread, so read it as a direction
rather than as a measurement.

The direction is coherent, and it is the same one §5 gives: two records break the cached prefix
twice and write more output, and output is priced at six times input. More compaction was not
less money.

Run 40 measured the other half. On `gpt-5.4-mini`, whose records are already complete, repeats
produced **negative** shrink on all three seeds (-1%, -4%, -2%), because a second record on a
model whose first one covered everything is duplication added to the prompt as preserved,
unshrinkable tokens.

So repeats help exactly when one record cannot cover the whole conversation, which is a property
of the model and the workload and not something a framework can know in advance. `UNCOVERED` is
the column that says which case you are in. **Off is the default because it is the setting that
cannot hurt**, and it also reproduces every run up to and including 39, so a row is comparable
with the archive unless it asks otherwise.

## 8. What is withdrawn

Stated plainly, because all of it reached a reader before it was checked.

**The runs 38-39 narrative is withdrawn.** It claimed the strategy had been destroying its own
record, and that this explained the archived `gpt-5.6-luna` result of 21/53. The mechanism does
not reach the archive: the code those runs executed dropped every tool group before the record
unconditionally and called the fallback only when the prompt still exceeded the ceiling, and
**every archived peak prompt sits 30-45% under its ceiling**, so the post-record fallback cannot
have fired in runs 26-35. What survives is narrower — `_anchored.py` genuinely had no concept of
a record, the run-38 trim was real, and protecting the record is a correct fix. It is not the
explanation of the archive.

Two further pieces of that narrative go with it. Run 39's "-2%, ranking above the control" is not
like-for-like: 77% fill against 85%, half the swing is the control moving +27%, and the instrument
printed `NOT SUPPORTED`; against run 34's control it is +25%. And "the first row in this project
to hold every fact without costing more" was false when it was written — run 33 had already done
it thirty lines earlier in the same document.

**"Runs 26-35 cannot be trusted because every row ran the unflagged fallback" is withdrawn.** It
was a large claim about a lot of paid-for work and it was wrong. Those runs are the cleanest
measurement of record-then-drop as designed, and the earlier explanation of the 21/53 — that
`gpt-5.6-luna` writes at length about the first two tool lookups of six and never reaches the
other four — stands.

**Six counting and arithmetic errors that reached a reader**, from
[`REVIEW-2026-09-06.md`](REVIEW-2026-09-06.md) §4:

## 9. Caveats on what is left

Named here rather than buried, because each of them limits a number quoted above.

**Run 42's cost axis is throttle-biased and is not claimed.** Five concurrent invocations of
eighteen strategies is about 4.5x the per-invocation load of run 41's five arms of four — roughly
2.7M tokens a minute against a 2M ceiling. 18 of 90 records were re-sent after rate-limit
refusals, **including the control in all five seeds**, which is the baseline every `vs none$` in
§4 is taken against; `tool_summary_anchored` was throttled in one seed of five. For the one
strategy that produced both kinds of seed, the throttled records cost 49% more than the clean one
on 4% more input (n=1, so suggestive rather than conclusive). The table prints
`VERDICT: tool_summary_anchored` at -6% with `NOT SUPPORTED` against a 37% spread; that verdict is
not claimed here. Throttling delays a request, it does not change what the model is sent or what
it writes, so `facts`, `acc1` and `snap%` are unaffected. The method note for the next run is that
concurrency has to be sized against strategies-per-invocation, not invocations.

**Runs 26-40 sit on the pre-fix output-reservation arithmetic and are not comparable with 41-42.**
Strategies size their input budget as `--context-window` minus `--max-output-tokens`, which the
runs set to 2,048 — but the value actually sent on the request was `--answer-max-tokens`, 12,000.
At a 60,000-token window the strategies believed 57,952 tokens of input were available when 48,000
were, so every threshold was a fraction of a budget overstated by about 10,000 tokens. Fixed on
6 September: ordinary calls now send what was reserved, and `--answer-max-tokens` goes on the
closing questions and on nothing else. **A cell taken before that fix and a cell taken after it
are two different instruments.** Do not put them in one table, and do not read a difference
between them as an effect.

**One run-41 arm seeded outside fill tolerance.** `luna, fixed payload` landed at 47,601 tokens
against a 51,600 target, **-7.8%**, and the instrument flagged it `FILL OFF TARGET`. The other
four arms are inside the 5% band — `mini-fixed` +4.2%, `mini-share80` +2.7%, and both share-0.80
luna arms -2.3%. Within an arm this changes nothing, since all four rows share one turn list and
one actual fill, so every `vs none$` in §3 stands; but that arm is not the 86% cell it is labelled
with and should not be read on the same axis as the others. Run 42's merged cell is -5.3%, also
flagged.

> An earlier statement of this caveat, carried in `RESULTS.md` and `STATE.md`, named `mini-fixed`
> at +5.3% and `luna-share80-repeats` at -6.5% as the two off-target arms. Recomputed from the
> archived records, neither figure exists: `mini-fixed` is +4.2% and `luna-share80-repeats` is
> -2.3%, both inside tolerance, and -5.3% is run 42's own merged fill.

**The two share-0.80 cells are not the same workload.** They carry matched tool results — 41,273
tokens on both — but different filler, so `gpt-5.6-luna`'s tool share is **84%** and
`gpt-5.4-mini`'s is **94%**. Cross-model reading at that cell is not like-for-like. Within-arm
comparisons, which is where every number in §3 comes from, are unaffected.

**Every `gpt-5.6-luna` row carries `NO:temp`.** The route rejected the temperature option and it
was dropped, so those rows are unpinned on temperature — and they also carry the largest spreads
in the project. No luna cost figure under roughly 40% should be read as meaning anything.

**Accuracy is a distribution, not a value.** Several rows in §4 carry a `seed+-` of 20 to 60
points. Both tables quote means over five seeds; a gap narrower than a row's own spread is not a
ranking.

## 10. What follows from this

**For a caller choosing a compaction setting.** Turn compaction on when the conversation would
otherwise exceed the window, and choose between strategies on retention, because that is the axis
where they actually differ. `context_window` — what `create_harness_agent` installs — lost more
than half the planted facts at the one cell where all eighteen were measured, and has not been
cheaper than not compacting in any cell of any run. Expect to pay for compacting; the question
worth asking is what you get for it. [`STRATEGIES.md`](STRATEGIES.md) is the per-strategy version
of that answer.

**For the next measurement, in order.**

1. **Sweep `--coverage-share`.** §6 is the largest unexplained variance left, the knob is newly
   reachable, and its effect on either axis is unmeasured.
2. **Re-take one archived cell on the post-fix arithmetic**, so the pre-fix archive has a bridge
   rather than being stranded.
3. **Size concurrency by strategies-per-invocation**, so run 42's cost axis can be re-taken
   cleanly.
4. **Move `--min-gain-fraction`.** `anchored` and `anchored_min_gain` differ in exactly that one
   value and have still only ever been compared at one setting of it.
