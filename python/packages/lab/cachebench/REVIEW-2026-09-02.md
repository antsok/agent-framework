# Adversarial review, 2 September 2026

Three reviewers were pointed at this work with instructions to attack it: one at the empirical
claims, one at the proposed strategy changes, one at the composite design and the upstream case.
They worked from the records rather than the write-ups. This file records what they found, what
was verified, and what it costs.

Everything below marked **verified** was re-derived independently before being accepted.

---

## 1. Two instrument defects, both affecting every cost number

### 1a. The control runs a different conversation from every strategy row — **verified**

| 120,000 / 0.86 | messages at peak | snapshot |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `none` | **82** | **100,278** |
| `anchored` | 109 | 105,672 |
| `anchored_min_gain` | 109 | 107,286 |

Same user-side turn list. The control carries **27 fewer messages**, and `anchored` — which is
inert at this cell, planning nothing — has a snapshot **5.4% larger**. Two rows that both did
nothing should be identical.

**Cause.** `filter_new_messages` (`_sessions.py`) falls back to `(role, serialized contents)`
for message identity when there is no `message_id`. Rows carrying a strategy get annotated and
always have ids; the control's byte-identical short acknowledgements to filler turns collide and
are dropped.

**This was explicitly dismissed** in `STATE.md` §6: *"affected nothing about the live runs — a
real model never repeats itself exactly."* The records refute it. The model repeats itself
constantly on filler turns.

**Direction:** the control is artificially cheap, so **compaction looks worse than it is**.

### 1b. `cost` includes twelve probe re-reads that scale with the snapshot — **verified**

`cost` sums seeding plus 12 probes per seed, and every probe re-sends the snapshot. A strategy
that compacts hard therefore receives a discount twelve times over on the *measurement* — one no
deployed agent receives, since an agent continues the conversation rather than being interrogated
twelve times from a frozen state.

Stripping the probe phase (probe input estimated as 12 x `prompt_tokens_final` at each row's own
hit rate):

| cell | `tool_summary_anchored` total | seeding only |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| share 0.80 / fill 0.86 | −14.1% | **−3.5%** |
| share 0.80 / fill 0.70 | −8.3% | **+14.3%** |
| share 0.60 / fill 0.86 | −4.2% | **+3.2%** |

Two of three flip sign.

**Direction:** **compaction looks better than it is.**

### What 1a and 1b do together

They push opposite ways on different cells, so no single correction rescues the axis. **Both
headline cost findings are compromised**: "compaction never pays" (biased by 1a) and "compaction
pays when tool output dominates" (created by 1b).

**What survives:** the #7912 before/after, because both arms carry both defects identically and
the paired difference holds. All three reviewers confirmed it independently.

## 2. A real bug in `MinimumGainAnchoredCompactionStrategy` — **verified**

The derivation defines `B` as the tokens *behind the edit*. The code compares the projected
saving against `included_token_count(messages)` — the **whole prompt**. Those differ by a factor
of roughly `prompt / B`.

Consequence: the floor is calibrated for a whole-band collapse near the band start, and is wrong
for an incremental collapse of a group that has just aged out of the tail, where the edit is near
the end and `B` is small. Measuring `B` from the plan — earliest shortened message to the end of
the included set — makes the test exact and is about ten lines.

**This supersedes the forward-projection proposal**, which was a threshold shift resting on a
diagnosis that turned out to be wrong.

## 3. My diagnosis of the min-gain reversal was wrong — **verified**

I reported that the floor declined gentle collapses, arrived at the ceiling untrimmed, and was
forced to shed whole groups.

The actual mechanism: `_keep_tokens_for` sets the per-result budget to
`ceiling x band_share / (tool groups in the band at that moment)`, and `_shorten` refuses to
re-trim a result already carrying the removal marker. **So a result is trimmed once, at whatever
budget was in force on the turn the trim happened to run, and frozen at that size.** Retention is
therefore path-dependent — a function of *when* the trim fired, not of the result or its
position.

That explains the 0.60/0.80 reversal without any shedding, and it is a worse defect than the one
I described: the module's opening docstring claims decisions do not depend on current size, and
this one does.

**No shedding has ever been observed.** In every recorded cell the peak prompt sits below the
input budget, so `_shed` has never executed live.

## 4. The freeze test passes for the wrong reason — **verified**

`test_decisions_are_frozen_as_the_conversation_grows` runs at `max_input_tokens=3_000`, where the
allowance `3000 x 0.25 / k` clamps to `DEFAULT_KEEP_TOKENS = 150` from k=6. Both conversations
trim to the same floor, so the assertion cannot fail. At a realistic ceiling — 117,952 gives
29,488 at k=1 and 4,914 at k=6 — the same test would expose the path dependence in §3.

## 5. `acc2` is a question-wording defect, and I cited it where it flattered

Across 240 control samples, **226 are exactly 1.0 or 11/53**. Eleven is the five non-tool facts
plus one code per lookup: the model reads *"every code returned by every deployment lookup"* as
*the* return code of each lookup. The scoped questions were rewritten to state their counts for
exactly this reason; the combined question never was.

And `tool_summary_anchored`'s 100% on `acc2` was reported as "the only row that ever beat the
control" — where a grouped record is being copied into an answer that asks for a grouped list.
The measure is rewarding the record's *format*.

## 6. Smaller corrections

- **"~20% tool share"** for the fixed-payload series is wrong: measured 43%, 37%, 26%, 21%, 26%,
  21% across the six cells. Fill and tool share moved together, so that series confounds them.
- **Cost variance is cache-miss episodes, not reply length.** Control uncached tokens per seed at
  60K/0.86: 117,006 / 68,853 / 68,439 / 74,201 / 68,189 — one seed lost 49,000 tokens to a cold
  cache while having the *lowest* output. The "under 20% is unresolvable" rule is wrong in both
  directions: the design is paired per seed, and paired differences resolve to 1–7 points.
- **The 0.23 default's empirical anchor is not on disk.** No record matches "263 tokens removed,
  92% → 88%, 46,471 re-read"; and per §1a that comparison was against a deficient control anyway.
- **#7918 is also compaction-relevant** and sits in the before/after range, so that comparison
  spans two relevant commits, not one.
- **STATE.md said "ninety" upstream commits**; the count is 50.

## 7. On the composite

Every threshold in it (0.60, 0.85, 0.50, 0.23) was fitted to cells already run, so measuring it
on those cells would prove nothing. Beyond that:

- The `tool_share >= 0.50` branch rests on the saving in §1b, which is mostly instrument.
- Its only **mandatory** branch — shed when over the ceiling — is code that has never run (§3).
- `tool_share` is endogenous: shortening lowers it, dropping groups behind a record collapses it,
  and the record itself counts toward it. A conversation can cross 0.50 in both directions on
  consecutive calls.
- Shedding to *just under* the ceiling moves the prefix every turn — the sliding-window behaviour
  measured at a 1–9% hit rate. The design needs hysteresis, which is exactly the trigger/target
  pair being requested upstream and not used here.
- Three size-dependent thresholds contradict the module's own first design constraint.

**Status: withdrawn pending a repaired instrument.**

## 8. On the upstream case

- The parity **facts** are right — `PipelineCompactionStrategy`, `CompactionTriggers` and
  trigger/target exist in `dotnet/` and in Go, and not in Python.
- But `docs/decisions/0019-python-context-compaction-strategy.md` Appendix B records a
  **deliberate** decision to make triggers strategy-internal and to reject the .NET model. The ask
  must argue against a recorded choice, not present an omission.
- The Learn page's cost premise is **incomplete rather than backwards**: cached tokens still cost
  something, and the caveat depends on strict-prefix caching, a discount above roughly 5x, a high
  hit rate, a long remaining horizon and no cache-write surcharge. Pitch it as an addition with
  the formula.
- **#7226 is largely delivered** — empty projection, destructive fallback, observability,
  `preserve_first_user_group`, bounded summarizer input, after-phase cadence — and what remains
  ("summarize before you delete") is the ladder this project's own data rates worst.
